our little story

We believe a loaf should take time, and a morning should taste like something kind.

Maison Plume began with one cast-iron oven, a stubborn sourdough starter named Olive, and a belief that the best things rise slowly. Today we're still small on purpose — flour-dusted hands, real butter, and recipes we know by heart.

meet the baker

Elodie keeps the oven warm.

Twelve years ago Elodie traded a city desk for a flour-dusted apron. She trained in a tiny Lyon boulangerie, came home, and built Maison Plume one loaf at a time. She still mixes the first batch before sunrise, because she swears the dough behaves better in the quiet.

  • Stone-milled, local grain. We work with two family farms an hour east.

  • No shortcuts, no improvers. Just flour, water, salt, butter and patience.

  • Yesterday's bread becomes tomorrow's good. Donated to the Linden food pantry, never wasted.

from starter to shelf

How a Plume morning unfolds

01

Feed Olive

9pm the night before, the starter gets fed and tucked in to bubble overnight.

02

Mix & fold

4am. Doughs are mixed by hand, then stretched and folded through the dark.

03

Shape & score

Each loaf is shaped, scored with a little wheat sheaf, and slid onto the stone.

04

Open at 7

Doors open, bells ring, the smell does the rest. Best to come early.

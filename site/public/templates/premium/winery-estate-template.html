Our Heritage

Four generations on the same slope.

In 1924, Elowen Marsh planted her first acre of vines where the morning fog lingers longest against the Calder ridge. She believed wine was not made but waited for — a conviction we have kept, stubbornly, ever since.

Today the cellar is run by her great-granddaughter, but little else has changed. We still pick by hand at dawn, still ferment with the wild yeasts that live in our barrel room, and still bottle only what the vintage earns.

Vine to Bottle

Patience, in four movements.

01

Tend

A full year in the vineyard — pruning, canopy work, and a single green harvest to concentrate the fruit.

02

Pick

Hand-harvested at dawn into small crates, sorted berry by berry on the old wooden table.

03

Ferment

Wild yeasts only, in open-top vessels, punched down by hand twice a day until the wine decides it is done.

04

Rest

Quiet months in barrel below ground, racked by candlelight, bottled only when the cellar agrees.

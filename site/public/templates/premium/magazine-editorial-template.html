Editor's Pick

The Hour Before Anyone Wakes

There is a particular hush that belongs to the hour before the household stirs, before the kettle and the inbox and the day's small negotiations begin. It is the only stretch of time I have found that resists being optimised. You cannot make it shorter without losing it entirely; you cannot fill it without spoiling its single, fragile virtue, which is emptiness. For years I treated this hour as wasted — a margin to be tightened, a slack to be cut. I have since come to believe it is the most useful part of the day precisely because it produces nothing, asks for nothing, and lets the mind drift toward thoughts it would never reach at noon. The slow read begins here, in the unhurried sentence that nobody is waiting for.
